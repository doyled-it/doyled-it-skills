# Session zero

## Overview

Session zero sets the table's expectations and safety before play, and it is the first place the
world and the players meet. Its two jobs for the loop are safety and seeds. Safety makes the
table one people want to return to. Seeds (each character's bonds and wants) are the raw
material the world ties itself to, so the game is about these characters, not a generic party.

Run it as a short real session with the players, not solo prep. It feeds `party-composition` and
`worldbuilding-initial-conditions` (finalize pass).

## Safety tools

Set these up explicitly. They are standard, and skipping them is a false economy.

- **X-Card.** Anyone can tap or raise the card to edit out content, no explanation owed. Agree
  on it out loud.
- **Lines and veils.** Lines are content that will not appear. Veils are content that happens
  off screen. Collect them privately (a shared anonymous doc) so no one has to out a limit in
  front of the group.
- **A consent checklist.** Use an established one (for example the RPG Consent Checklist or the
  broader safety toolkits). It surfaces topics people would not raise unprompted.
- **An open-door policy.** Anyone can step out or raise a concern between sessions.

Record the agreed lines and veils where you will see them during prep. They are a hard input to
every later artifact.

## Table expectations

Agree on the boring, game-saving logistics:

- Tone and genre dial (gritty to heroic, grounded to gonzo), and how lethal the game is.
- Schedule, session length, and attendance expectations.
- Table rules: phones, metagaming, PvP, spotlight sharing, how disagreements get resolved.
- The pitch: give the one-paragraph premise from the world premise pass, enough to build
  characters who belong in the setting.

## Character bonds and wants

For each player character, collect and write down:

- **One bond:** a person, place, or piece of unfinished business that ties the character to the
  world. These become NPCs, locations, and hooks.
- **One want:** what the character is chasing. These point the strong start and reward choices.
- Optionally, one connection to another player character, so the party has a reason to be a
  party.

These are the seeds. worldbuilding-initial-conditions (finalize pass) ties at least one villain
or NPC to a bond or want. session-prep points the strong start at a want.

## Outputs (artifacts handed forward)

In Obsidian (the default binding), the player-facing handout is marked with the Player Facing Notes
`pfn` flag, and the bonds and wants feed PC notes that become Initiative Tracker party members, per
`../dungeon-master-loop/references/obsidian-ttrpg-formats.md`.

- Agreed safety tools, and the table's lines and veils (a hard constraint on all prep).
- Table expectations and tone.
- Per-character bonds and wants, feeding the party roster, the world finalize pass, and hooks.

## Common mistakes

- Treating session zero as optional for a one-shot. Even a one-shot needs safety tools and one
  bond and want per character.
- Collecting limits out loud, so people self-censor. Gather lines and veils privately.
- Gathering bonds and wants and then not tying the world to them, which wastes the seeds.
