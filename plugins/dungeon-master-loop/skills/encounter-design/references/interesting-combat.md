# Interesting combat: the shared core (cited checklist)

Difficulty tuning lives in `encounter-math-2024.md`. This file is the other half: what makes a
fight fun rather than a slog. Both encounter-design (planned) and improvised-combat (at the
table) use this. Claims are attributed; items marked unverified came from secondary summaries.

## Give the fight a question, not a kill count

- State the fight's dramatic question to yourself as a yes or no ("Can the party hold the bridge
  until the gate closes?"). The fight ends when the table knows the answer. (The Angry GM, "Four
  Things".)
- Replace "kill everything" with an objective: escort, steal, cross, hold, assault, escape,
  survive a timer, protect an NPC, disrupt a ritual. Tell the players the objective; players who
  do not know why they are fighting disengage. (Burn After Running; Sly Flourish.)
- Add a pressure source: a timer, reinforcement waves (weaker foes first, stronger later), or a
  collapsing structure. (Sly Flourish.)

## Terrain does real work

- Use one or two meaningful features, not many. Too many elements slow the action. (Burn After
  Running.)
- Reach for difficult terrain, cover enemies can use too, chokepoints (a bridge, a doorway), and
  height (archers on a ledge). (Burn After Running.)
- Add interactive objects or monuments in larger battles. (Sly Flourish.)
- Put hazards on the battlefield with clues, so players can play around them. Isolated gotcha
  traps are usually unsatisfying. (Burn After Running.)

## Enemy tactics and roles

- Mix roles with synergy: brutes in front, ranged and controllers behind, a few lightning rods
  that let the party land big hits. (Sly Flourish.)
- Scale tactics to Intelligence: about 7 or less acts on instinct, 12 or more coordinates, 14 or
  more assesses and exploits weaknesses. (Keith Ammann, via excerpt, unverified against the book.)
- Scale retreat to Wisdom: 7 or less flees too late, 8 to 11 flees but picks targets poorly, 14 or
  more fights only when confident. (Ammann, via excerpt, unverified.)
- Plan advance, fight, and retreat before combat, often with an HP threshold for retreat. Nearly
  every creature wants to survive (constructs and undead are the usual exceptions). Prefer ambush,
  sniping, and withdrawal when the statblock supports it.

## Action economy, bosses, minions

- Do not send one monster against a whole party; solo monsters die fast, and the gap widens at
  higher level. (Sly Flourish.)
- Give a boss extra actions. Colville-style villain actions sit on top of the normal action
  economy and often follow a pattern across rounds (position, then escape, then explode).
  Legendary actions and legendary resistance also work. (Sly Flourish on Colville.)
- Use support or summoned minions that reposition freely and burst late in the fight. Spend
  complex statblocks on bosses and lieutenants; run minor foes simply. (Sly Flourish.)

## Pacing

- Keep most fights short. Long battles leave the roleplay and exploration players behind. (Sly
  Flourish.)
- Watch the decision points: once most players are out of meaningful choices, you have about
  three turns before the fight becomes a chore. Ending early beats dragging. (The Angry GM.)
- Change the situation mid-fight: add a wave, collapse a floor, let the villain speak. Narrate
  from the characters' point of view. (Sly Flourish.)
- Mix a few prepped set pieces with quick improvised filler. Spend prep on the set pieces.
