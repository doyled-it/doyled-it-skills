# Encounter difficulty for D&D 5e 2024 (cited, edition-labeled)

Every number here is labeled with its source and edition. Spot-check the XP values against your
copy of the 2024 Dungeon Master's Guide before relying on them, because the printed table could
not be opened directly during research.

## The 2024 method

The 2024 DMG builds a combat budget in three steps:

1. Pick a difficulty band: Low, Moderate, or High. (2014's Easy and Deadly are gone, and these
   are not a straight rename.)
2. Look up the XP budget per character for the party's level (2024 DMG, chapter 4).
3. Multiply by the number of characters, then spend that budget on monsters at their listed XP.

The 2014 encounter multiplier and the "adjusted XP" step are removed. Source: D&D Beyond's own
2024 DMG walkthrough (which works a level 5 Moderate example: 750 XP per character, so a party of
five has 3,750 XP), plus Blog of Holding and an EN World table post. Blog of Holding notes the
2024 budgets track the 2014 ones up to about level 9, then rise faster.

per-level table in the book as canonical.

## Party size

The budget is linear: it is per character, multiplied by the count. A sixth character adds one
more per-character share. Budget for the party that actually shows up, not the party on the
sheet. The 2024 DMG has a Troubleshooting section for odd cases (a missing player, many weak
creatures); its text was not read here, so consult it directly.

## Party makeup: qualitative adjustments, not official math

The 2024 DMG gives no numeric adjustment for composition. These are judgment calls layered on the
XP budget. (Attributed to Sly Flourish and Blog of Holding.) The reason the flat multiplier was
dropped is exactly this: area damage punishes swarms, single-target control punishes solo
monsters, so a count-based multiplier was wrong.

- Action economy: more bodies on a side shift a fight at identical XP. A lone big monster dies
  fast to a full party.
- Area damage and control: a party with strong area damage deletes swarms; strong control neuters
  a solo boss. Against such a party, prefer resilient mid-sized groups and spread threats out.
- Healer and defensive tools: factor in whether the healer is reachable, and answers to legendary
  resistance.
- Weak saves: a party soft in a save (often Wisdom) is exposed to charm, fear, and stun. Use
  those deliberately, not by accident.
- Nova versus endurance: a burst party wins short fights and fades across a long day. A party low
  on resources plays a tier harder; Blog of Holding suggests chaining two or three High
  encounters when one feels too easy.
