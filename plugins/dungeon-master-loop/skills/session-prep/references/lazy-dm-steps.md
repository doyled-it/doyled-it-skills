# Lazy Dungeon Master prep: the eight steps (cited)

The session-prep backbone is Mike Shea's (Sly Flourish) eight steps of lazy prep. The current
wording is from "The Eight Steps of the Lazy DM: 2023 Review" (published 2023-10-23), which
reviews the method from Return of the Lazy Dungeon Master (2018). The order is unchanged from
2018. The only change in the 2023 review is to step 4.

| # | Step | Produces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Review the Characters | Each PC's wants and connections, plus a possible hook |
| 2 | Create a Strong Start | An opening event that pulls players into play |
| 3 | Outline Potential Scenes | A few brief, flexible scenes that might happen |
| 4 | Define Secrets and Clues | Short relevant secrets, discovery left for play |
| 5 | Develop Fantastic Locations | The places scenes might happen, in minimal detail |
| 6 | Outline Important NPCs | Names and key details of NPCs who might appear |
| 7 | Choose Relevant Monsters | Monsters that fit likely locations, placed flexibly |
| 8 | Select Treasure and Magic Item Rewards | Loot chosen or rolled randomly |

2023 change to step 4: instead of writing ten fresh secrets every time, carry forward secrets
that are still relevant and not yet uncovered. This carry-forward is what the loop's
`advancing-the-world` step 4 implements.

The steps are modular. Pick and combine them for a one-shot or a campaign, homebrew or published
material.

## The Lazy GM's Resource Document

A free companion (license: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, so reuse and modification are
allowed with attribution; it also carries SRD 5.1 material). It adds, beyond the eight steps:

- A 5-minute reduced checklist, and example strong starts.
- Forty secrets-and-clues prompts across four categories (character, historical, NPC or villain,
  plot), with the advice to keep a secret separate from where it is discovered.
- A session zero checklist, safety tools, character connection tables, and quest templates.
- Quick encounter building by level and a deadly-encounter benchmark table (levels 1 to 20).
- Random generators (NPCs, treasure, traps, travel, chambers, town events, and tables for strong
  starts, quests, Fronts, locations, names, relationships).

## Shorter and complementary frameworks

- Shea's "Refined 15-Minute D&D Prep" collapses to three roughly five-minute steps: a strong
  start, three to five evocative locations, and up to ten secrets and clues sorted by type. These
  map onto steps 2, 5, and 4.
- The Alexandrian's node-based design preps situations, not plots: nodes (people, places,
  events) linked by information via an inverted Three Clue Rule, prepping only through the next
  layer.

## What is consensus versus Sly-Flourish-specific

Consensus across frameworks: prep situations and options not scripted plots; secrets and clues
are a core output, decoupled from where they are found; prep only what you will likely use; a
strong opening beat and evocative locations recur everywhere.

Specific to Sly Flourish: the fixed eight-item order, character-first review as a formal step,
carry-forward secrets (the 2023 change), and the D&D-specific benchmarks and generators in the
Resource Document.
