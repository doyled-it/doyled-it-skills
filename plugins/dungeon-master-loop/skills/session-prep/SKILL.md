# Session prep

## Overview

This is the world-to-session half of the loop. It reads the current world state and produces one
runnable session plan. It is deliberately minimal: prep the ten percent the table will touch,
and improvise the rest. The backbone is the Lazy Dungeon Master's eight steps (Mike Shea), which
are modular and ordered so a session comes together in well under an hour. See
`references/lazy-dm-steps.md` for the cited step list and what each produces.

Prep situations, not plots. A session plan is a set of possibilities, not a script. The players
decide the order.

## Inputs

Before prepping, confirm the world is current: `advancing-the-world` ran for the last session.
Then pull the party roster (party-composition), the table safety limits (the lines, veils, and
tone agreed at session-zero), the Front and faction clocks, the secrets list, and hooks and
seeds. The safety limits are a hard constraint on every scene, NPC, and monster you prep.

## The eight steps

Work them in order. Skip any that this session does not need.

1. **Review the characters.** Re-read the party roster. Note each character's current want and
   the spotlight owed (from the spotlight checklist). Pick a hook pointed at a want.
2. **Create a strong start.** Open in the middle of something happening, tied to a player's want
   or an advancing clock. Not a briefing.
3. **Outline potential scenes.** A short list of scenes that might happen. Loose, reorderable,
   not a sequence. Include at least one combat, one social, and one exploration or puzzle option
   so the session is not all one texture (see non-combat below).
4. **Define secrets and clues.** Pull from the rolled-forward list and add new ones, about ten
   total, written as standalone facts not tied to a place. Apply the Three Clue Rule to any
   conclusion the session needs.
5. **Develop fantastic locations.** For each place a scene might happen, three evocative details
   and any interactive or hazardous terrain. Terrain is a combat lever (see encounter-design).
6. **Outline important NPCs.** Names and one line each for NPCs who might appear. Flesh out on
   demand. Tie back to bonds, the Front, and factions.
7. **Choose monsters.** Pick monsters that fit the locations, placed flexibly. For a designed
   fight, invoke `encounter-design`. For a monster the books lack, invoke `custom-monsters`.
8. **Select rewards.** Loot and magic items, at least one tied to a player's want, plus one that
   hints at the larger world.

Gate (Phase 5 in the loop): a session plan with a strong start, secrets in play, and at least
one designed challenge.

## Non-combat challenges

Challenges and triumphs are not only fights. Design social, exploration, and puzzle scenes with
the same care as combat. Read `references/non-combat-challenges.md` for the full checklist. The
core moves:

- Give the scene a dramatic question (a yes or no the table is trying to answer), a conflict, and
  real stakes. The scene ends when the question is answered.
- Offer multiple valid approaches (talk, sneak, lore, magic, muscle, craft) and make each
  viable. List at least three per scene.
- Never resolve a whole scene on one roll. Track a changing state (an NPC's patience, a guard's
  alertness, a progress clock) that the fiction moves.
- Fail forward: a failed roll changes the situation (a clock ticks, a cost is paid, an attitude
  shifts), it does not dead-end the session.
- Use the spotlight checklist: make sure each character has at least one scene built for them.

## Prep-time write-back

Prepping invents things: a new NPC to voice, a location you sketched, a secret you decided is
true, a reskinned monster. Do not strand these in the session note. Record each one in a "Prep
inventions" list in the session plan. That list is the exact field `advancing-the-world` Step 2
reads. After the session it promotes the ones the party engaged with into canon, and it parks the
ones the party never reached as hooks or iceberg seeds rather than discarding them. Either way the
world updates during prep, not only after play, and no prep invention is left to die in the
session note.

## Outputs (artifacts handed forward)

A session plan: strong start, potential scenes, secrets in play, fantastic locations, NPCs,
chosen or designed monsters, rewards, the non-combat challenges, and the "Prep inventions" list.
encounter-design and improvised-combat consume its fights; printable-minis-and-terrain consumes
its enemies and terrain; advancing-the-world consumes its Prep inventions list and recap after
play.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a scene sequence (a plot) instead of a scene menu (a situation).
- An all-combat session. Build at least one social and one exploration or puzzle beat.
- Resolving social or exploration scenes on a single skill roll.
- Inventing an NPC or location in prep and never recording it in the Prep inventions list, so it
  never reaches canon or a hook.
